The projects below look different on the surface. They share a recurring move: make the hidden object or interface explicit, then ask what follows from that choice and what fails when the interface collapses distinctions.

ML foundations · neural architecture

Architecture Before the Formula

Develops an individuation problem for neural architecture: two descriptions can preserve the same predecessor information while differing in represented organization or downstream accessibility. The paper studies architecture before quotienting everything to a composite input-output map.

Mechanistic interpretability · foundations

Mechanistic Localization Is Distribution-Relative

Shows that a fixed network and fixed receiver support need not determine one receiver-level mechanism. The realized probability law can change the optimal rule while leaving the structural support unchanged, separating predictive adequacy from context invariance.

Scientific ML · 3D cell lineage inference

From Detection to Identity

A public case study in scientific inference: local visual evidence is separated from accepted biological identity, missing annotation from negative evidence, motion accuracy from uncertainty calibration, and local relation scores from globally compatible lineage structure.

Current questions

What I keep coming back to.

I am interested in research problems where a technically correct solution can still miss the prior question: what exactly has been represented, individuated, localized, optimized, or warranted?

  • When do two internal computations count as the same architecture or mechanism?
  • Which distinctions remain available after a representation, routing interface, or measurement has been fixed?
  • How should mechanistic claims change across distributions and contexts?
  • What happens to scientific inquiry when machines can produce results faster than human communities can integrate them?
  • Who directs scarce automated research capacity when execution becomes increasingly scalable?
